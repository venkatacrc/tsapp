@title ఈ కోర్సు ఎలా వాడాలి · How to use this course
@summary The staircase (సోపానం) from letters to stotras, what each kind of box means, and a daily routine that makes it stick.

## సోపానం — a staircase, one step at a time
This course climbs from what you already know to what you want to read. Every step uses the one below it, so go in order the first time; later, jump around freely.

1. **అక్షరమాల · Script and sounds.** Telugu and Devanagari are sister scripts with the same alphabet order. You will read Devanagari in a day, and learn exactly which Telugu letters Sanskrit does not use (ఎ, ఒ, ఱ…) and which sounds a Telugu speaker must adjust.
2. **రెండు భాషల వంతెన · The bridge.** How Telugu grammar works (the way Chinnaya Sūri's బాలవ్యాకరణం taught it) set side by side with Sanskrit grammar: genders, cases, verbs and sentences. Then two lessons that list what only Telugu has and what only Sanskrit has.
3. **మొదటి వాక్యాలు · First sentences.** Everyday spoken Sanskrit: introducing yourself, home, food, time, requests and short dialogues.
4. **Nouns, pronouns, numbers, verbs, participles.** Every declension class and every tense and mood (లకారం), with full tables, each one tied to a Gītā or Sahasranāmam line where it appears.
5. **సంధి, సమాసం, తద్ధితం, అవ్యయం.** Joining sounds, compounds, secondary suffixes and indeclinables: the tools that make stotra lines look impossible until you can take them apart.
6. **వాక్య నిర్మాణం, ఛందస్సు.** Kāraka (who does what to whom), case rules, verse word order (అన్వయం) and metre.
7. **శ్లోక పఠనం · Readers.** Graded readings from subhāṣitas to the Gītā, the Viṣṇu Sahasranāmam and the stotras, each read the traditional Telugu way: పదచ్ఛేదం → అన్వయం → ప్రతిపదార్థం → తాత్పర్యం.
8. **జ్ఞాపకం · Memory.** Techniques, cheat sheets and a study plan.

## The boxes you will see
> [telugu] తెలుగులో :: How the same idea works in Telugu. Start from here: it is the part you already know.

> [compare] Telugu ↔ Sanskrit :: A direct comparison, often a trap where Telugu habit gives the wrong Sanskrit.

@nocards
> [memory] Memory aid :: A trick, rhyme, chant or picture to make a rule stick. Every titled memory aid also becomes a flash card.

> [sutra] Pāṇini's sūtra :: The rule as Pāṇini stated it in the అష్టాధ్యాయీ, with its number. You do not have to memorise sūtras, but they are short and chantable, and many teachers quote them.

> [warning] Watch out :: A common mistake.

> [note] Note :: Extra detail you can skip on the first reading.

Worked examples show the Sanskrit in your chosen script with IAST underneath, then the word split (పదచ్ఛేదం), prose order (అన్వయం), word-by-word meaning (ప్రతిపదార్థం), the Telugu and English meaning, and grammar notes. Tap **🔊** to hear it read aloud (by your phone's or Mac's Hindi or Telugu voice: an approximation, not a pandit).

## Choosing your script
Settings let you show Sanskrit in **తెలుగు లిపి** or **देवनागरी**, with the other script and IAST underneath if you like. Start with Telugu script so nothing slows you down; switch to Devanagari after Unit 1 so that you can read any Sanskrit book. Lessons about the scripts themselves always show both.

## Cards, Quiz and Lipi
- **Cards.** Every table row, worked example and titled memory aid becomes a flash card. *Learn* shows the whole card; *Test* hides the answer until you tap. Cards you get right come back after 1, 3, 7, 16 and then 35 days; cards you miss come back tomorrow. Choose **Due today** to practise only what needs reviewing.
- **Quiz.** Multiple-choice questions for each lesson, with an explanation for every answer. Your best score per lesson is kept.
- **Lipi (లిపి).** Type or paste Sanskrit in Telugu script, Devanagari or IAST and see it in the other two. Useful for reading Devanagari books and for typing Sanskrit with a Telugu keyboard.

## A daily routine
@nocards
| Time | What to do |
|---|---|
| 5 min | Cards → *Due today* in Test mode. Never skip this, even on busy days. |
| 15–20 min | One new lesson section. Read it aloud, Sanskrit lines especially. |
| 5 min | Copy one table by hand from memory, then check it. |
| 5 min | Chant the lesson's verse or paradigm until you can say it without looking. |
| Weekly | Take the quizzes of the week's lessons; redo any below 80%. |

> [memory] Say it aloud, write it once, test it tomorrow :: Sanskrit was memorised by sound for three thousand years. Chanting a table aloud (రామః రామౌ రామాః…) uses the same memory that keeps film songs in your head. Writing it once fixes the spelling. Testing it the next day, before you have forgotten it completely, is what moves it to long-term memory.

@quiz
q: In what order should you take the units the first time?
* In order: script, the bridge, first sentences, then grammar, then readers
- Start with the Gītā readers and look up grammar as needed
- Any order: the units are independent
why: Each step builds on the one below it. After one pass you can jump around.

q: What does the Cards "Due today" option show?
* Only the cards whose review date has come, based on your earlier answers
- Every card in the course
- Only cards from today's lesson
why: Correct answers push a card's next review further out (1, 3, 7, 16, 35 days). Missed cards come back the next day.

q: Which reading order do the Readers follow, the same order Telugu commentaries use?
* పదచ్ఛేదం → అన్వయం → ప్రతిపదార్థం → తాత్పర్యం
- తాత్పర్యం → పదచ్ఛేదం → అన్వయం
- అన్వయం → తాత్పర్యం → ఛందస్సు
why: Split the sandhi, put the words in prose order, give word meanings, then the overall meaning.
@end
