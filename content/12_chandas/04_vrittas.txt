@title వృత్తాలు · The classical metres of the stotras, and Telugu metres
@summary The fixed-pattern metres (vṛttas) of the dhyāna ślokas and stotras: శార్దూలవిక్రీడితం, మందాక్రాంత, వసంతతిలక, శాలిని, తోటకం, భుజంగప్రయాతం, స్రగ్విణి, పంచచామరం, మాలిని, స్రగ్ధర. Each with its gaṇas and a verse you may already know. Then Telugu's ఉత్పలమాల, చంపకమాల, శార్దూలం and మత్తేభం compared, and the two big differences: ప్రాస and యతి.

> [memory] Hear the metre in the name :: Many names are self-describing. भुजङ्गप्रयात "the gliding of a snake" (।ऽऽ ।ऽऽ: ta-TUM-TUM), तोटक (।।ऽ: ta-ta-TUM, a trotting beat), मन्दाक्रान्ता "slow-stepping" (long run of heavies, then a run of lights), शार्दूलविक्रीडित "the tiger's play" (a long, bounding line). Chant one verse of each and the pattern sticks.

## Telugu metres and their Sanskrit cousins
| Telugu metre | Gaṇas | Syll. | Telugu yati | Sanskrit relation |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| ఉత్పలమాల | భ ర న భ భ ర వ (లగ) | 20 | 10th | a Sanskrit-pattern vṛtta used mostly in Telugu |
| చంపకమాల | న జ భ జ జ జ ర | 21 | 11th | a Sanskrit-pattern vṛtta used mostly in Telugu |
| శార్దూలం | మ స జ స త త గ | 19 | 13th | = शार्दूलविक्रीडित |
| మత్తేభం | స భ ర న మ య వ (లగ) | 20 | 14th | = मत्तेभविक्रीडित |
| కందం | 4-mātrā gaṇas | — | — | close to Sanskrit आर्या (a mātrā metre) |
| తేటగీతి, ఆటవెలది, సీసం | సూర్య and ఇంద్ర gaṇas | — | — | Telugu ఉపజాతులు; no Sanskrit equivalent |

> [compare] ప్రాస: Telugu has it, Sanskrit doesn't :: Every Telugu వృత్తం needs ప్రాస: the second syllable's consonant must be the same in all four lines. Sanskrit verse has no such rule; the Gītā's lines rhyme only by accident.

> [compare] యతి means two different things :: Telugu యతి is a sound-harmony (యతిమైత్రి) between the first letter of a line and the letter at the yati position (e.g. the 13th in శార్దూలం). Sanskrit यति is just a pause (caesura) at a fixed position, ideally at a word boundary. When you recite a Sanskrit శార్దూలం, pause after the 12th syllable; when you write a Telugu శార్దూలం, match the 1st and 13th letters.

## How to identify an unknown metre
1. Count syllables per quarter: 8 → anuṣṭubh; 11 → triṣṭubh family; 12 → jagatī family (toṭaka, bhujaṅga, sragviṇī, vaṃśastha); 14 → vasantatilakā; 17 → mandākrāntā; 19 → śārdūla; 21 → sragdharā.
2. Scan one quarter and split it into gaṇas.
3. Check a second quarter: in a vṛtta all four should match.
