## A year through the course
The plan assumes 30 minutes a day, six days a week. With 15 minutes, take two years; with an hour, nine months. The order is the order of the lessons: each unit uses the ones before it.

> [memory] One milestone a month :: Write the twelve milestones on a card and tick them off. A month that ends without its milestone is not a failure: repeat it. Slow and complete beats fast and patchy, because every unit is built on the one before.

## Daily routines
@nocards
| సమయం | రోజువారీ పని |
|---|---|
| 15 minutes | 5 min: flash cards due today. 7 min: one section of the current lesson, read aloud. 3 min: recite one table or verse from memory. |
| 30 minutes | 10 min: flash cards due today. 15 min: the current lesson, with its worked examples and quiz. 5 min: recite your anchor verses (lesson 14.1). |
| 60 minutes | 15 min: flash cards. 25 min: the current lesson. 10 min: one verse from unit 13, split and parsed in a notebook. 10 min: chant a table or a stotra aloud. |

> [memory] Never skip the cards :: If you have only five minutes, spend them on the cards due today. The lessons can wait a day; the spacing of the reviews cannot.

Once a week, take a day off from new material: redo the quizzes of the last three lessons, and recite everything you have memorised. On that day also write out one verse from memory with its anvaya and word meanings.

## Habits that make the difference
- **Read aloud.** Sanskrit was made to be heard. Every table, rule and verse goes in faster through the ear and mouth than through the eye.
- **Keep a notebook.** For each verse you study: the verse, the split, the anvaya, each word with its form (e.g. "रामाय: राम, m, 4th sing."), and the meaning in Telugu.
- **Parse everything.** For every word you read, ask: noun or verb? Which case, number, gender, or which person, number, lakāra? Once this is a habit, reading becomes easy.
- **Use your Telugu.** When a word looks familiar, check the Telugu meaning against the Sanskrit one (lesson 14.1 lists the false friends).
- **Recite with a group.** If there is a Gītā or Sahasranāma group near you, join it. Group chanting is the pāṭhaśālā method, and it works.

| V. S. Apte, *The Practical Sanskrit-English Dictionary* | the best dictionary for Classical texts | daily reading |
| Monier-Williams, *A Sanskrit-English Dictionary* | the largest dictionary, with roots and sources | for rare words |

> [note] Online tools :: The Gita Supersite of IIT Kanpur has the Gītā with several commentaries and translations. sanskritdocuments.org has stotras in many scripts, including Telugu. The Sanskrit Heritage site (Gérard Huet) can split sandhi and parse words automatically: use it to check your own parsing, not to replace it.

> [note] Speaking :: Saṃskṛta Bhāratī runs short spoken-Sanskrit camps (सम्भाषण शिबिर) in many cities and online. Ten days of speaking does more for fluency than months of reading.

## What "all the rules" covers in this course
This course sets out to teach the whole grammar of Classical Sanskrit, the language of the Gītā, the purāṇas, the stotras and kāvya. It covers:
- the script and every sound, with the Telugu–Devanagari mapping;
- every major noun class and the irregular nouns, all pronouns and numbers;
- all ten lakāras, both padas, the ten gaṇas, passives, the four derived verbs and the 22 prefixes;
- all participles, absolutives, infinitives and gerundives, and the main kṛt and taddhita suffixes;
- all vowel, consonant and visarga sandhi rules you will meet in texts;
- every type of compound and how to unpack long ones;
- the kārakas, the words that govern cases, absolute constructions and anvaya;
- the main metres of the Gītā and the stotras.

It leaves out some things on purpose:

| వదిలేసినది | ఎందుకు | ఎక్కడ నేర్చుకోవాలి |
|---|---|---|
| Vedic accent (उदात्त, अनुदात्त, स्वरित) and Vedic grammar (लेट्, Vedic infinitives, old forms) | not used in Classical texts; Vedic recitation is learned from a teacher | a veda pāṭhaśālā; Macdonell's *Vedic Grammar for Students* |
| full Pāṇinian derivation (प्रक्रिया) of every form, sūtra by sūtra | the course quotes key sūtras but teaches patterns, which is enough for reading | लघुसिद्धान्तकौमुदी, then सिद्धान्तकौमुदी |
| the forms of all roots | there are about 2000 roots; the course teaches the patterns and 30 key verbs | a dhāturūpa book |
| rare and archaic forms (e.g. rarely used aorist and perfect forms, some irregular nouns) | you will meet them once in a long text | Kale's grammar, a dictionary |
| poetics (अलङ्कार, ध्वनि, रस) | it is a subject of its own, built on top of grammar | Kuvalayānanda, Kāvyaprakāśa |
| the rarer metres and Vedic metres | the course covers those of the Gītā and the common stotras | Vṛttaratnākara |

> [memory] Enough to read, then the rest by reading :: The goal of the course is that you can pick up the Gītā, a stotra or a purāṇa and read it with a dictionary. Everything left out is best learned in the way the tradition learned it: by meeting it in texts you love.

* the flash cards due today
- a new lesson
- a quiz you have already passed
- read a new text
why: The spacing of reviews is what makes memories last. A new lesson can wait a day; due cards should not.

q: Which book is the traditional collection of noun tables?
* शब्दमञ्जरी
- अमरकोशः
- लघुसिद्धान्तकौमुदी
- वृत्तरत्नाकरः
why: शब्दमञ्जरी is the book of declensions. अमरकोशः is a thesaurus, लघुसिद्धान्तकौमुदी a grammar and वृत्तरत्नाकरः a book on metre.

q: Which of these does this course leave out?
* the Vedic pitch accent
- visarga sandhi
- the dual number
- the aorist
why: The Vedic accent is not used in Classical Sanskrit and is learned from a teacher in veda study. The other three are taught in full.
