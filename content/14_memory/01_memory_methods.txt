@summary Why Sanskrit grammar is easier to remember than it looks, the methods of the pāṭhaśālā (chanting, kārikās, Pāṇini's own compression tricks), the methods of memory research (spaced repetition, active recall, interleaving), how the app's six review boxes work, and an anchor verse for every major rule.

## Sanskrit was built to be memorised
For two thousand years Sanskrit grammar was learned without books. Pāṇini wrote his grammar for memory, and the tradition built a whole set of techniques around it. You can use the same ones.

| Technique | What it is | Where you met it |
|---|---|---|
| सूत्र | rules compressed to a few syllables | every [sutra] tip |
| प्रत्याहार | one syllable naming a whole group of sounds (अच्, हल्, यण्) | lesson 1.9 |
| इत् markers | silent letters that carry information (डुकृञ्) | lessons 6.2, 13.11 |
| अनुवृत्ति | a word stated once is understood in the rules that follow | below |
| कारिका | a verse summary of a topic | lessons 6.2, 6.6, 12.2 |
| शब्दमञ्जरी / धातुमञ्जरी | chanting noun and verb tables daily | lesson 4.1 |
| कण्ठपाठ | learning verses by heart before analysing them | unit 13 |

An example of अनुवृत्ति: इको यणचि (6.1.77) says "इ उ ऋ ऌ become य व र ल before a vowel". The next rule, एचोऽयवायावः (6.1.78), says only "ए ओ ऐ औ become अय् अव् आय् आव्". It does not repeat "before a vowel": अचि carries over from the rule before. Pāṇini's 4000 rules are short because most of their words are inherited this way.

> [memory] Brevity is the point :: Grammarians say they rejoice at saving half a short vowel as much as at the birth of a son (अर्धमात्रालाघवेन पुत्रोत्सवं मन्यन्ते वैयाकरणाः). Every rule in this course has been reduced to something you can say in one breath. Say it aloud; don't just read it.

## The pāṭhaśālā method: chant, then understand
1. **Chant the table.** Say each noun table in rows of three (singular, dual, plural), case by case: रामः रामौ रामाः · रामं रामौ रामान् … Chant each verb table in rows of three, person by person: भवति भवतः भवन्ति · भवसि भवथः भवथ · भवामि भवावः भवामः.
2. **Chant the kārikā.** Learn the summary verses by heart: the ten classes (भ्वाद्यदादी …), the ten lakāras (लट् वर्तमाने …), the anuṣṭubh rule (पञ्चमं लघु सर्वत्र …).
3. **Learn a verse that uses the rule.** A rule without an example fades. A rule tied to a verse you can recite stays.
4. **Only then analyse.** Understanding comes faster when the forms are already in your mouth.

> [telugu] You did this already :: Telugu schools teach the vibhakti list by chanting: "డుముపులు ప్రథమా విభక్తి, నిన్ నున్ లన్ గూర్చి గురించి ద్వితీయా విభక్తి…". You still remember it decades later. The Sanskrit tables work the same way: chant them until they are as automatic as డుముపులు.

## What memory research adds
| Method | What to do | Why it works |
|---|---|---|
| spaced repetition | review a card just before you would forget it, at growing gaps | each successful recall makes the memory last longer |
| active recall | cover the answer and produce it yourself; then check | recalling strengthens memory far more than re-reading |
| interleaving | mix card types (nouns, verbs, sandhi, verses) in one session | you learn to recognise which rule applies, not just the rule |
| elaboration | connect each new form to something you know (Telugu, a verse) | connected memories are easier to find |
| sleep | review before sleeping; test the next morning | memories are consolidated during sleep |
| little and often | 20 minutes daily beats 3 hours on Sunday | spacing is built into the habit |

## How the app's flashcards work
Every table row, worked verse and memory aid in the course becomes a card. The app keeps each card in one of six boxes:

@nocards
| Box | Next review | How a card gets there |
|---|---|---|
| 0 | tomorrow | new, or answered wrongly |
| 1 | after 1 day | answered right once |
| 2 | after 3 days | right twice in a row |
| 3 | after 7 days | right three times, or marked "Known" in Learn mode |
| 4 | after 16 days | right four times |
| 5 | after 35 days | right five times; it stays here while you keep answering right |

> [memory] Be honest with the button :: The boxes only work if you mark a card wrong when you hesitated or guessed. A wrongly marked "right" sends a weak card away for weeks. When in doubt, press wrong: it costs you one extra review tomorrow.

A good daily routine with the app:
1. **Due today** first: clear every due card (10–15 minutes).
2. **New** cards from the lesson you are studying (5–10 a day is plenty).
3. **Marked for review**: the cards you flagged as hard.
4. Once a week, take the quizzes of the last few lessons again.

## The Telugu bridge as a memory tool
About half of literary Telugu vocabulary is Sanskrit (tatsama or tadbhava). Use it:
- **Know the stem from Telugu.** Telugu రాముడు, వనము, సీత, హరి, గురువు, రాజు, మనస్సు give you the Sanskrit stems राम, वन, सीता, हरि, गुरु, राजन्, मनस्. The Telugu ending even tells you the stem type (lesson 0.2).
- **Use Telugu compounds.** దేవాలయం, విద్యార్థి, మహాత్ముడు, పద్మనాభుడు are Sanskrit compounds. Analyse the ones you already know before learning new ones.
- **Use Telugu names.** Every friend called శ్రీనివాస్, హృషీకేశ్ or జనార్దన్ is a Sahasranāma name and a compound type (lesson 13.8).
- **Watch for false friends.** Telugu meanings sometimes drift: Telugu "సంసారం" means family life, but Sanskrit संसार is the cycle of births; Telugu "ఉపన్యాసం" is a speech, Sanskrit उपन्यास is a statement or proposal.

## A memory palace for the cases
If you like visual memory, put the eight cases in the rooms of a house you know well (your childhood home in Andhra or Telangana works perfectly):

| Room | Case | Image |
|---|---|---|
| front door | प्रथमा (who) | Rāma standing at the door: रामः |
| verandah | द्वितीया (whom, where to) | he looks at the forest: वनं पश्यति |
| kitchen | तृतीया (with what, by whom) | cooking with a ladle: दर्व्या |
| pūjā room | चतुर्थी (for whom) | an offering: कृष्णाय नमः |
| back door | पञ्चमी (from where) | someone runs out: गृहात् निर्गच्छति |
| cupboard | षष्ठी (whose) | father's box: पितुः पेटिका |
| bedroom | सप्तमी (where, when) | sleeping in the room: गृहे स्वपिति |
| window | सम्बोधन (calling) | calling out: हे राम |
